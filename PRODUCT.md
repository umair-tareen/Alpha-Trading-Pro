# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Aspiring and early-stage retail traders, roughly beginner to intermediate, who are tired of Discord-alert culture and "buy this now" guru hype. They have usually lost money following other people's calls and want to learn to trade for themselves with discipline. Their context when they land here: evaluating whether to invest 3,400 to 6,900 dollars in real 1-on-1 mentorship, skeptical of gurus, looking for proof that this teaches skill rather than selling dependency. A second, smaller audience wants the software only: traders who want the Sigma Terminal without any mentorship.

## Product Purpose

One-on-one trading mentorship that teaches decision-making, risk management, trade management, psychology, and a repeatable process, so the student ends up trading independently instead of following alerts. Three mentorship packages (stocks, stocks plus options, stocks plus options plus futures) plus a separate monthly software subscription, the Sigma Terminal, for people who want the platform on its own. Success looks like a visitor who distrusts trading gurus by default coming away thinking "this person actually teaches, and is honest about risk."

## Brand Personality

Disciplined, candid, expert. The voice is plain-spoken and anti-hype: risk before profit, process before setups, independence before dependency. It should feel like a seasoned trader who has blown accounts, learned the hard lessons, and now teaches the boring fundamentals that actually compound. Emotions to evoke: confidence, trust, seriousness, and relief at finally finding a real teacher. Never: urgency, FOMO, or get-rich-quick.

## Anti-references

- Crypto and forex guru sites: lambos, watches, lifestyle marketing, screenshots of fake P&L.
- Discord-alert communities: "buy this," "watch this stock," neon green everything, countdown timers.
- Purple-gradient AI-SaaS landing pages: the Inter font, the bento grid, the gradient hero text, the identical icon cards.
- Navy-and-gold "trust me, I am basically a bank" fintech cliche.
- Editorial-magazine aesthetic (display serif, italic, drop caps): wrong register for a trading mentor.
- Any fabricated proof: invented testimonials, made-up student counts, fake win rates. We do not have permission to claim these and will not.

## Design Principles

1. Practice what we preach. The design carries the same discipline and restraint we teach. Calm beats loud. Nothing on the page is hype the content cannot back up.
2. Earn trust by what we do not claim. No fake stats, no fabricated screenshots. Honesty is the differentiator in a category full of liars.
3. Teach, do not sell. Clarity over persuasion. Show the process and the curriculum; let substance do the convincing.
4. Edge through substance. The content is the proof. Depth and specificity outweigh decorative polish.
5. Independence. The page should empower the reader and read as confident, not needy. We are choosing students, not begging for them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Body text at or above 4.5:1 contrast, large text at or above 3:1. Full keyboard focus states. Honor prefers-reduced-motion with a static fallback for every animation. Semantic heading order. No information carried by color alone (the red/green trading contrast is always paired with an icon or label).
